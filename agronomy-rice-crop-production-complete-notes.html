Rice Crop Production (Complete Notes)

Agronomy · Cereal Crops · Notes for BSc/MSc/PhD, AFO, JRF/NET & state exams

On this page: Basic facts · Area & production · Races · Climate & soil · Seasons · Varieties · Seed rate · Fertilizer & N · Biofertilizers · Water · Deficiencies · Weeds · Beushening & puddling · Dapog · Hybrid rice · Golden rice · SRI · Processing · Memory chain

1. Basic facts

Rice (Oryza sativa) is the staple food of more than 60% of the world's population; about 90% of the world's rice is produced and consumed in Asia. It is a crop of the wet tropics, also grown in humid subtropics. In India, rice holds the first position among cereals in both area and production.

  • Protein ≈ 6–7% (lowest among cereals); fat ≈ 2–2.5%, much of it lost in milling
  • Hulling percentage ≈ 66% (about two-thirds of paddy)
  • Water need: cereals generally 400–500 L per kg dry matter; rice ≈ 5000 L per kg grain
  • Self-pollinated; fruit is a caryopsis; stem is called haulm/culm (nodes + internodes); inflorescence is a panicle (group of spikelets)
Exam pointer Rice = C3, short-day, self-pollinated, caryopsis, panicle — this cluster appears as a one-liner in nearly every exam.

3. Classification and geographic races

Genus Oryza has 24 species; two are cultivated — O. sativa (diploid, 2n = 24, three geographic races) and O. glaberrima (West Africa only).

RaceKey characteristics
IndicaTropical/subtropical; tall, weak stem, elongated grains, droopy leaves, lower productivity, less input-responsive
JaponicaTemperate/subtropical Japan; short stature, erect dark-green leaves, highly input-responsive, highest productivity; oval–round grains
Javanica (Bulu)Indonesia; few tillers, wild type, many awns, long duration

4. Origin, climate, soil and growth

  • Origin: Indo-Burma region (South-East Asia)
  • C3, short-day plant; hot, humid climate; average growth temperature 21–37 °C
  • Best soils: clay and clay loam; tolerates acidity — suitable pH 4–6
  • Semi-aquatic: grows submerged; oxygen moves to roots through aerenchyma tissue

6. Varieties — the facts exams love

  • TN-1 (Taichung Native-1), Taiwan 1964–65 — first dwarf variety in the world and first introduced variety in India
  • IR-8 = Peta × Dee-gee-woo-gen (IRRI, 1966) — first high-yielding dwarf, photo-insensitive; called "Miracle rice" (introduced from IRRI, Manila); Dee-gee-woo-gen = "brown tipped short legged"
  • Jaya — Miracle rice of India
  • Cuttack (Odisha): Malinja & Mahsuri · Mutant dwarfs: Jagannath, Sattari
  • Direct seeding: Bala, Cauvery, Nagina-22, Pusa-2-21 · BLB tolerant: TKM-6
  • Salinity tolerant: IR-8, Lunishree · Waterlogged: Jalmagan, Jalmani
  • First Basmati hybrid in world: PRH-10 · First hybrid rice in India: CORH-1 (1994)
  • IARI releases include Norin-8, IR-18, Pusa 2-21, Basmati-370, Pankaj, IR-20; Pusa Basmati-1 — convergent breeding
  • Genome-edited varieties: DRR Dhan 100, DST Rice-1
Exam pointer TN-1 = first dwarf; IR-8 = miracle rice; Jaya = miracle rice of India. Examiners love swapping these three.

8. Fertilizer and nitrogen management

General NPK for high-yielding rice: 100:60:60, with ZnSO₄ 25 kg/ha at last puddling.

  • Waterlogged puddled soil = thin oxidized zone above + reduced zone below
  • Avoid nitrate fertilizers in lowland paddy — heavy losses by leaching and denitrification; if used, place in the oxidized zone
  • Deep placement of ammoniacal N in the reduced zone is the efficient method
  • Nitrogen-use efficiency in rice: 30–40% (IARI, Ph.D.-1997)

9. Biological nitrogen fixation

  • Algal biofertilizers can cut rice N requirement by ≈25–30%
  • BGA and Azolla (with Anabaena azollae) fix nitrogen in lowland rice
  • Azotobacter — non-symbiotic fixer for upland rice

10. Water management

  • Critical stages: tillering (≈2–20 DAT) and booting / late panicle initiation — the most critical stage
  • Booting = swelling of the flag-leaf sheath around the panicle
  • ≈5 cm standing water is beneficial during reproductive and grain-formation stages

11. Nutrient deficiencies and toxicities

Khaira disease

  • Cause: zinc deficiency, usually at nursery stage; first reported by Y. L. Nene, 1966, Pantnagar
  • Symptoms: leaf tips yellow then dry; stunted plants; rusty brown-red colour on older leaves
  • Management: ZnSO₄ at nursery (5 kg) or 0.5% foliar spray early

Browning of rice

  • Iron toxicity — waterlogged acid-sulphate soils; purple leaf tips → scorched look; Phalguna is tolerant

Silicon

  • Regarded as essential in rice; deficiency → soft, droopy plants
  • Needed most around panicle initiation; cuticle–silica layer resists insect attack
  • Correct with basic slag 1.5–2.0 t/ha (calcium silicate source)
Exam pointer Khaira = Zn · Browning = Fe toxicity · Akiochi = H₂S toxicity · White-eye = Fe deficiency. Make this a flashcard.

12. Weed management

Major weeds: Echinochloa crus-galli (barnyard grass) and E. colona. Weeds are a bigger problem in upland direct-seeded rice than in transplanted lowland rice.

13. Beushening and puddling

  • Beushening: cross-ploughing the young crop 4–6 weeks after sowing with a light plough in 5–10 cm standing water — for weed control, stand optimisation and aeration
  • Puddling: breaking soil aggregates mechanically at excess moisture — reduces percolation losses, reduces weeds, increases nutrient availability
  • Disadvantage: destroys soil structure and hampers roots of the following upland crop

14. Dapog method

  • Introduced from the Philippines; mat-type nursery without soil contact
  • Sprouted seed at ≈3 kg/m² on planks, trays, concrete or polythene-covered beds
  • Seedlings ready in ≈12 days; nursery of 25–30 m² serves one hectare

15. Hybrid rice

  • First developed in China (late 1970s) using the CMS–fertility restoration system; India was second to commercialise
  • Examples: APRH-1, APRH-2 (AP), KRH (Karnataka), MGR-1 (TN); PHB-71 — private-sector hybrid

16. Golden rice

Term coined by Ingo Potrykus; transgenic rice enriched with beta-carotene to raise vitamin A.

17. System of Rice Intensification (SRI)

  • Agro-ecological approach of Henri de Laulanié, Madagascar, 1980s
  • Principles: less irrigation, lower plant population, better soil condition, minimal chemicals, organic manures
  • Yields 6–7 t/ha vs 2–3 t/ha conventional; up to 90% seed saving and 50% water saving
  • 25 × 25 cm spacing; 8–12-day (two-leaf) seedlings; nursery ≈100 m²; seed ≈5 kg/ha
  • Drawback: higher initial labour (single-seedling transplanting, weeding)

18. Processing of rice

  • Milling: removes aleurone layer and embryo — loss of vitamin B₁ (thiamine); deficiency → beriberi
  • Polishing: removal of fine bran from milled rice, smoothing the surface
  • Parboiling: hydrothermal process; crystalline starch → amorphous by irreversible swelling and fusion

Note: one printed question table gives "Vitamin B₁₂" for parboiling while the processing text discusses vitamin B₁ (thiamine) — verify against your exam's answer key.

19. Rapid memory chain

  • Rice → Oryza sativa → 2n=24 → self-pollinated → caryopsis → panicle → C3 → short-day → semi-aquatic
  • TN-1 → first dwarf · IR-8 → high-yielding dwarf "Miracle rice" · Jaya → Miracle rice of India
  • Khaira → Zn · Browning → Fe toxicity · Akiochi → H₂S toxicity
  • Dapog → Philippines → mat nursery → ~12 days → 25–30 m²/ha
  • Puddling → less percolation → fewer weeds → more nutrients
  • SRI → 25×25 cm → young seedlings → less seed & water → more initial labour
