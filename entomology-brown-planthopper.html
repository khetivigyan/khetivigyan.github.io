Brown Planthopper

Entomology · Insect Pests of Rice · Notes for BSc/MSc/PhD, AFO, JRF/NET & state exams

Brown planthopper (BPH), Nilaparvata lugens (Hemiptera: Delphacidae), is a sap-sucking pest found at the base of rice plants.

Damage: Both nymphs and adults suck phloem sap from the base of tillers, causing circular patches of dried, burnt-looking plants — "hopperburn". BPH also transmits grassy stunt and ragged stunt viruses.

Management: Avoid excess nitrogen and close planting; drain water from the field; maintain alleyways (30 cm every 2–3 m); conserve mirid bugs and spiders; grow resistant varieties; need-based sprays of pymetrozine or buprofezin directed at the plant base.
