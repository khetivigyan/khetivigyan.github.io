Stem Borer

Entomology · Insect Pests of Rice · Notes for BSc/MSc/PhD, AFO, JRF/NET & state exams

Yellow stem borer (Scirpophaga incertulas, Lepidoptera: Crambidae) is the most serious borer pest of rice in India, especially in lowland and deep-water rice.

Damage symptoms: Larvae bore into the stem and feed on the growing point. In the vegetative stage this kills the central shoot, producing the classic "deadheart". Attack after panicle initiation produces empty, whitish panicles called "whiteheads", which can be pulled out easily.

Life cycle: Eggs are laid in masses near leaf tips, covered with buff-coloured hairs from the female's anal tuft. Larval period 25–35 days with 5–6 instars; pupation inside the stem. Several overlapping generations occur in a season.

Management: Clip seedling leaf tips before transplanting to remove egg masses; harvest close to the ground and destroy stubble; use pheromone traps (5/ha) for monitoring; release egg parasitoid Trichogramma japonicum; grow tolerant varieties; apply need-based insecticides such as cartap hydrochloride or chlorantraniliprole when deadheart crosses ETL (5% deadhearts or 1 egg mass/m²).
